## A verdict that names no part

- When a client cannot say what is wrong, break the design into components and narrow down which one is not working; ask for reasons ("because") rather than a bare dislike. A List Apart, "I Don't Like It" (alistapart.com/column/i-dont-like-it/) and "Design Criticism and the Creative Process" (alistapart.com/article/design-criticism-creative-process/) [named expert]
- Name the candidate parts to probe: the colour palette, the layout, the images, something else. Dribbble, "How to avoid a bad feedback loop" (dribbble.com/stories/2020/02/06/how-to-avoid-bad-feedback-loop) [unverified]
- Keep asking why, what or how until the answer is concrete: "not exciting" became "the imagery looks flat". Envato Tuts+, "How to handle negative feedback from design clients" (webdesign.tutsplus.com) [unverified]
- Vague feedback is a main cause of project delay and overrun. Interaction Design Foundation, "How to get meaningful design feedback from your clients" (ixdf.org) [named expert]
- Read at search-excerpt depth only (2026-11-26); no study tests the tactic. The default — break the work into its parts and have the client point at one — rests on the two [named expert] sources, with the other two agreeing.
