Researched: 2026-11-26

## A verdict that names no part

- When a client cannot say what is wrong, "break the design into components and try to narrow down which part isn't working for them"; don't take "I don't like it" at face value. Laura Kalbag, "I Don't Like It", A List Apart, 17 July 2014, https://alistapart.com/column/i-dont-like-it/ [named expert]
- "For likes and dislikes ask specifically about typography, color, layout, images, etc."; "Ambiguity feels safer but it doesn't benefit the conversation or the design." Cassie McDaniel, "Design Criticism and the Creative Process", A List Apart, 11 January 2011, https://alistapart.com/article/design-criticism-creative-process/ [named expert]
- Have the client walk the mockup and point out what they like and don't, and why; start with basic styles and elements. Suzanne Scacca, "How to Handle Negative Feedback from Design Clients", Envato Tuts+, 22 February 2024, https://webdesign.tutsplus.com/how-to-handle-negative-feedback-from-design-clients--cms-108494t [named expert]
- The default — list the work's parts and have the client point at the one that is off, in their own words — rests on all three; no study tests it.
