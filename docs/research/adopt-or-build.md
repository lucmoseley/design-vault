# Research: adopt expert design guidance, or write the house's own? (design-vault#119, map #96)

⚠ **Bound 14 Oct 2026 (#101): whether to buy Baymard Premium goes to lucmoseley/mantahostels with its search pages, and whether Manta ships a dark theme is Manta's own ruling. Small-business trust, rated WRITE here, goes to research #144 first.**

Date: 13 Oct 2026.

Question (from #119): which expert-built design guidance must the house adopt or use, and not write itself? Is impeccable's guidance good enough that the house's job is mostly to enforce it more strictly?

Binding condition (#96 Notes, "One system, not a Frankenstein"): each source is judged on two more points. (1) Does it state the conditions and trade-offs behind its rules? (2) Is it one coherent system, designed as a whole, or a list of independent tips?

Source strength labels (same as #104):
- **Primary**: the standard, the spec, or the first-party owner of the claim (W3C, web.dev, Apple, Google Material, GOV.UK, Baymard, an NN/g study).
- **Strong secondary**: an established expert reference (Butterick, Refactoring UI, Bringhurst, Every Layout), or a peer-reviewed paper.
- **Weak**: blog, vendor marketing, opinion, star counts.

Inputs already known and not redone: `docs/research/impeccable-checks.md` (#97), `docs/research/design-coverage-gaps.md` (#104), and the mantahostels files `candidate-design-skills.md`, `installed-design-plugins-inventory.md`, `kowalski-skills-fit.md`, `haines-marketing-skills-fit.md`, `cole-skills-sweep.md`.

## F1. impeccable: who writes it, its licence, how fast it changes, and its stated basis

- **Author.** Paul Bakaus (`plugin.json` author; README "Created by Paul Bakaus"). His own page names his work: he made jQuery UI (2007), led Chrome DevTools product and developer relations at Google (2013-2016), then AMP and Web Stories developer relations, then Google for Creators; EVP Product at Spotter (2022-2024). He calls himself a "Product engineer". He names no design training and no design credential. He founded Renaissance Geek, the company behind impeccable; it raised a seed round led by a16z and bundles impeccable with the GitHub Copilot app. Source: https://paulbakaus.com/about/ (read 13 Oct 2026). **Primary** (first-party, about himself).
- **Contributors.** `pbakaus` has 1,808 commits; the next human has 216, then 10 or fewer (GitHub API `contributors`, 13 Oct 2026). It is in effect one author's work. **Primary.**
- **Repository and licence.** https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable, Apache-2.0, created 16 Nov 2025, 80,493 stars, 4,808 forks, 77 open issues, last push 12 Oct 2026 (GitHub API). **Primary.** Apache-2.0 lets the house copy and change its text, with attribution and a change notice.
- **Release speed.** 32 `skill-v*` releases from 28 Apr 2026 (3.0.4) to 9 Oct 2026 (4.5.2). From 4 Sep to 9 Oct 2026 there were 10 skill releases (4.2.0 to 4.5.2) and 12 detector-engine releases (0.1.3 to 0.1.14). The repository has about 2,422 commits; about 556 of them since 12 Sep 2026 (GitHub API `releases`, `commits` page count). **Primary.** Thus a rule can change week by week. #97 found that 4.2.1 and 4.5.0 have identical `critique.md` and `craft-floor.md`, so the judging text is more stable than the build flow.
- **Stated basis.** The README says: "Anthropic's frontend-design was the first widely-used design skill for Claude. Impeccable started from there", and its motive is the "tells" of model output ("Inter for everything, purple-to-blue gradients, cards nested in cards, gray text on colored backgrounds"). The README names no design research, standard or expert source (README read 13 Oct 2026; grep for "research", "source", "based on": no basis statement). A grep of all 4.5.0 skill files for named authorities finds only one citation: "Miller's Law revised by Cowan, 2001" (`critique.md` line 338). WCAG is named as a level ("WCAG AA"), never by criterion. `android.md` defers to "Material Design 3". **Primary** (the files).
- **Judgement.** impeccable is an expert *engineer's* opinionated distillation, built to correct model habits. It is not a design system made by a design team, and it does not cite the evidence behind its rules. Its strength is that it is written for an agent, it is current, and it is machine-readable. Its weakness, for #119, is provenance: a critic cannot tell an impeccable rule that comes from evidence from one that is the author's taste. Confidence 0.85.

## F2. A sample of 42 impeccable rules, checked against primary and expert sources

Method. I read impeccable 4.5.0 `typeset.md`, `colorize.md`, `layout.md`, `animate.md`, `clarify.md`, `craft-floor.md`, `audit.md` (§1-5) and the numeric lines of `adapt.md` and `optimize.md`, plus `critique.md` lines 318-345. Detector rule text comes from the engine 0.1.11 binary strings. For each rule I found the expert or primary source that owns the claim.

Verdict key:
- **R** = right, and impeccable states the conditions (or the rule needs none).
- **RU** = right in its core, but stated as universal where the expert source states conditions, a range, or a trade-off; or it is the author's taste with no source found.
- **D** = dated: a convention from an earlier era.
- **W** = wrong, misapplied, or contradicted by a primary source.

**Pattern.** (1) Where impeccable restates a standard (WCAG, Core Web Vitals, NN/g forms and dialogs), it is right; but it never names the criterion, so a critic cannot check the edge (2.5.8 spacing exception, 2.2.2 pause control, 2.4.11 focus not obscured). (2) Where it states a craft value (measure, leading, durations), it is right and usually inside the expert range. (3) Where it bans a pattern (eyebrow, side border, Inter, nested cards), the rule is a reaction to 2024-2026 model output, not a design principle; at least one ban (eyebrow) contradicts an expert system that conditions the same pattern. (4) Its one cited piece of science (Cowan) is misapplied in a way that hurts a search-and-filter site most. (5) The old `adapt.md` text is the most dated file. Confidence 0.8 overall; 0.6 for rule 6.

## F3. impeccable as a system: does it state conditions, and is it coherent?

- **It has one real condition frame.** Every axis file starts with "Visitor mode": Persuade and Experience may let display type and motion "carry the voice"; Operate and Read put "stability, scanability, and measure" first (`typeset.md`, `animate.md`). The craft floor's Refuse list says "These are the category's defaults, not bans: the brief's own words can earn any of them". A pinned brief "overrides anything here". This is the same split that IBM Carbon makes between its productive (dense product UI, 14px base) and expressive (web page, 16px base) type sets (F2 rule 1). **Primary** (the files).
- **But the conditions stop at the mode.** Inside a mode most rules carry no trade-off. Examples from F2: the ≤4-options rule (38) has no condition for search and filter tasks; the eyebrow ban (23) is "a ban, not a default: no brief earns it back"; the theme rule (17) has no user-preference condition; the touch-target rule (34) has no spacing exception.
- **It contradicts itself across files.** Measure 45-75ch (`typeset`) against 65-75ch (`craft-floor`); "combine size, weight, space, and tone" (`typeset`) against a detector that measures size alone; content-driven breakpoints against "640, 768, 1024px" in the same `adapt.md`; and #97 F10 found the detector's font list does not match `new-work.md` §4. These are drift between files, not designed trade-offs.
- **It names no conflict between its own rules.** #97 F8 found conflicts are resolved by fixed precedence (brief > floor > habit), and no reviewer is asked to name a clash.
- **Two kinds of rule sit in one list.** Craft rules (measure, contrast, durations, states) mostly agree with an expert source, though impeccable cites none (F2 rules 1-9, 12-16, 25-33, 39-42). Anti-"AI look" bans (eyebrow, side border, Inter, nested cards, gradient text) are taste of the 2024-2026 era, and the detector enforces them with the same weight as a contrast failure. A critic that enforces impeccable "more strictly" enforces both kinds equally unless the house labels them.
- Judgement: impeccable is **coherent enough to be a spine** (one author, one condition axis, one vocabulary) and **not coherent enough to be enforced as-is** (unconditioned rules, cross-file drift, taste and evidence mixed with no label). Confidence 0.8.

## F5. Mixing rules from several sources: where it is safe and where it is not

A rule from a coherent system depends on the rest of that system. Carbon's 14px body works because Carbon's productive set, IBM Plex, its 2x grid and its density were designed together (F2 rule 1). GOV.UK's 10px left border on inset text works because GOV.UK uses almost no other decoration, and the page warns that "Some users do not notice inset text" on complex pages (F2 rule 22).

Three kinds of rule, and the mixing risk of each:

1. **Constraints and evidence: safe to mix.** WCAG 2.2 criteria, Core Web Vitals limits, behaviour findings (NN/g, Baymard), perception (Gestalt grouping, irradiation), typographic ranges (measure, leading). These limit outcomes; they do not pick a look. Two of them can conflict only on priority, not on style. Each must carry its own condition (Baymard's split view is for accommodation results; NN/g's hamburger finding is about navigation of more than four items). Confidence 0.85.
2. **Behaviour patterns at component level: safe to mix when the look is stripped.** How a form validates, how an error summary links to fields, when inset text is wrong, when to use a confirmation versus undo. GOV.UK, USWDS, NN/g and Baymard agree closely here, because they all come from user testing. Take the *behaviour and its condition*, never the source's visual values. Confidence 0.8.
3. **Value systems: not safe to mix.** Type scale, spacing scale, radius scale, elevation and shadow recipe, motion curves and durations, colour-role map, and taste bans. Take these from **one** source per project: the project's own `DESIGN.md` and brand sheet. A value may come from an outside system only as a *whole token family* (for example, Manta's easing and durations match Material 3's standard scheme as a set; F4a), never one value at a time. Taste bans from different packs contradict each other: mantahostels found that taste-skill's `minimalist-skill` bans the Lucide-style icons the Manta brand sheet requires (`installed-design-plugins-inventory.md` §3), and impeccable bans the eyebrow that GOV.UK and Anthropic's `frontend-design` allow with a condition (F2 rule 23, F4c). Confidence 0.85.

**Precedence when two good sources disagree** (proposed, for the founder's sites):
1. Law and WCAG 2.2 AA (a floor nobody waives).
2. The project's own rules file, brand sheet and `DESIGN.md` (the one value system; it may override anything below and must record why).
3. Domain evidence for the task at hand (Baymard for accommodation search, NN/g for navigation and forms, GOV.UK and USWDS for form behaviour).
4. Expert craft ranges (Butterick for type; M3 and NN/g for motion timing; Every Layout for intrinsic layout).
5. impeccable's craft rules (the spine; F2 shows they mostly sit inside 4).
6. impeccable's and other packs' anti-"AI look" bans (era-bound taste; a default the brief can earn back, with no exception, including the eyebrow).

It also keeps the system one system: rules in levels 1, 3 and 4 constrain; only level 2 chooses the look.

## F6. Per area: the best source and the verdict

Verdicts:
- **ADOPT**: take an outside source as the rule, cite it by name and section; the house writes only the check question.
- **ENFORCE**: impeccable (or another source) as it is, checked more strictly by the critic or a tool.
- **EXTEND**: keep the source as the spine; the house adds a sourced correction, condition or missing rule.
- **WRITE**: no adequate outside source; the house (or the project) writes its own, from evidence where it exists.

## Answer

**Is "stricter enforcement of impeccable" the right core?** Partly. It is the right **spine for craft**, and the wrong **whole**.

Why it is the right spine:
- In a sample of 42 rules, 26 (62%) are right with their conditions, and 11 (26%) are right in their core but unconditioned. Only 3 (7%) are wrong and 2 (5%) are dated (F2). On typography, colour, layout, motion timing, states and copy it agrees with Butterick, WCAG, NN/g and Material 3.
- It is current, Apache-2.0, written for an agent, and it has one condition axis (visitor mode) that matches how expert systems split dense from expressive work (F3).
- No other agent skill is a better spine (F4c). Writing the house's own craft rules would mostly restate impeccable with less maintenance behind it.

Why enforcing it as-is, more strictly, is wrong:
1. **Strictness amplifies its errors.** The ≤4-options rule would mark a hostel filter bar as "overloaded" (F2 rule 38). The absolute eyebrow ban, the theme rule and the loop rule would be enforced against better-conditioned sources (F6 conflicts).
2. **It mixes evidence and taste with one weight.** The anti-"AI look" bans are 2024-2026 fashion; enforced as hard rules they date fast and fight the brief (F3).
3. **It cites nothing.** A critic cannot tell an impeccable rule that has evidence from one that is taste, so it cannot judge an edge case (F1).
4. **It changes weekly.** 10 skill releases in five weeks (F1). Strict enforcement needs a pinned version and a diff on each upgrade.
5. **It misses whole areas.** WCAG 2.2 by criterion (2.4.11, 2.2.2, 2.5.8, 3.3.7, 3.3.8); accommodation search UX (Baymard); the familiar-structure trade-off; trust and fee disclosure; SEO structured data; Gestalt by name; a method for surface styling (#117); a distinctive-asset method (F6; #104).
6. **It never reads the project's rules file** (#97 F6), and the founder's rejections are mostly taste no rule held (#102: 3 of 28 breached a written rule). Stricter rule enforcement, from any source, reaches a small part of what he rejects. Calibration against his verdicts (#96 Notes) does more.

**Recommended shape (one system; F5's six levels, with levels 5 and 6 merged into level 5):**
- Level 1, floor: **ADOPT** WCAG 2.2 AA by criterion and Core Web Vitals; **ENFORCE** them by tool (axe-core, Lighthouse) and by named critic questions.
- Level 2, look: one value system per project (`DESIGN.md`, brand sheet). It may override anything below and must record why. The house writes no generic style values.
- Level 3, domain evidence: **ADOPT** Baymard for accommodation search, NN/g for navigation and behaviour, GOV.UK and USWDS for form and component behaviour (Markdown on GitHub, free licences, conditioned).
- Level 4, expert craft ranges: Butterick for type, NN/g and Material 3 for motion timing, Every Layout and web.dev for layout; cited, not copied.
- Level 5, spine: **ENFORCE** a pinned impeccable, with a short house override file that (a) strikes or corrects F2 rules 6, 23, 29, 36, 37, 38; (b) adds conditions to the RU rules; (c) labels the anti-"AI look" bans as era-bound defaults, with a review date; and (d) cites the level-1, 3 or 4 source behind each enforced rule.
- **WRITE** only where no outside source exists: imagery's information rule, the trust and disclosure checklist, the distinctive-asset method, brand-motion physics (the craft gate already does this), and the surface-styling method (#117).

What this does not settle: whether to buy Baymard Premium; whether Manta ships a dark theme; how the override file is pressure-tested. Those go to the founder or to #121, #100 and #101, which this ticket blocks.
