## Part 1. Source re-checks

### R1. W3C Understanding SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum), with techniques G18 and F83 (P)
- URL: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum, read by curl 19 Oct 2026. Page footer: "Date: Updated 06 September 2026." WCAG 2.2 date: first a W3C Recommendation 5 Oct 2023; the TR page read today (https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) is dated "W3C Recommendation 12 December 2024" (the 2024 edition with errata).
- "The visual presentation of text and images of text has a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1, except for the following: Large Text Large-scale text and images of large-scale text have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1; Incidental Text or images of text that are part of an inactive user interface component , that are pure decoration , that are not visible to anyone, or that are part of a picture that contains significant other visual content, have no contrast requirement. Logotypes Text that is part of a logo or brand name has no contrast requirement." CONFIRMED.
- Large scale (key term): "with at least 18 point or 14 point bold or font size that would yield equivalent size for Chinese, Japanese and Korean (CJK) fonts". Note 1: "Fonts with extraordinarily thin strokes or unusual features and characteristics that reduce the familiarity of their letter forms are harder to read, especially at lower contrast level[s]". CONFIRMED.
- "'18 point' and 'bold' can both have different meanings in different fonts but, except for very thin or unusual fonts, they should be sufficient." CONFIRMED.
- "1pt = 1.333px , therefore 14pt and 18pt are equivalent to approximately 18.5px and 24px". CONFIRMED.
- Inactive: "User Interface Components that are not available for user interaction (e.g., a disabled control in HTML) are not required to meet contrast requirements. An inactive user interface component is visible but not currently operable." CONFIRMED.
- Outlined letters: "A narrow border around the letter would be used as the letter. A wide border around the letter that fills in the inner details of the letters acts as a halo and would be considered background." CONFIRMED.
- G18 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G18, read 19 Oct 2026): for a ground light on one edge and dark on the other, "there is no color that can run across the page and meet the contrast guidelines on both edges"; fixes are to change the letters' lightness "so that each letter always meets the contrast ratio for the background that is immediately behind the letter", or "to provide a halo around the text". Example: "the area behind the text is fogged white so that the picture is very faint". CONFIRMED.
- F83 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/failures/F83, read 19 Oct 2026) Quickcheck: test "the contrast between the text and the area of the image that is darkest (for dark text) or lightest (for light text)". CONFIRMED.
- Reading: COL-1 (4.5:1 body, 3:1 at 24 px or 18.5 px bold; thin or unusual faces are harder to read at low contrast, but the ratio itself does not change for them). COL-11 (measure at the worst pixel behind the text; fixes are a halo, a fog or panel, or letter colour that follows the ground). COL-65 (disabled controls are exempt).

### R3. W3C Understanding SC 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast and SC 2.4.13 Focus Appearance (P)
- URLs: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast and .../focus-appearance, read by curl 19 Oct 2026. Both "Date: Updated 06 September 2026."
- 1.4.11 (AA): "The visual presentation of the following have a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 against adjacent color(s): User Interface Components Visual information required to identify user interface components and states , except for inactive components or where the appearance of the component is determined by the user agent and not modified by the author; Graphical Objects Parts of graphics required to understand the content". CONFIRMED.
- Boundaries: "If a control has visible content (such as text or a sufficiently contrasting icon), which helps users identify the presence of the control, then a border or other indication of the overall boundary of the hit area is not required". "Having a visual boundary indicating the hit area is only required when there is no other visual way to identify the presence of the control". "Even when a control does not need to have a visual boundary indicating its hit area, it will still need to have a sufficiently contrasting focus indication." "For people with cognitive disabilities, it is a best practice to delineate the boundary of all control[s]". CONFIRMED.
- States: "any visual information necessary to indicate state, such as whether a component is selected or focused must also ensure that the information used to identify the control in that state has a minimum 3:1 contrast ratio." "This success criterion does not require that changes in color that differentiate between states of an individual component meet the 3:1 contrast ratio when they do not appear next to each other. For example, there is not a new requirement that visited links contrast with the default color, or that mouse hover indicators contrast with the default state." CONFIRMED.
- Thresholds and thin lines: "2.999:1 would not meet the 3:1 threshold". "Due to anti-aliasing, particularly thin lines and shapes of non-text elements may be rendered by user agents with a much fainter color than the actual color defined in the underlying CSS"; "best practice would be for authors to avoid particularly thin lines and shapes, or to use a combination of colors that exceeds the normative requirements". CONFIRMED.
- Focus indicators: outside the component it "needs to contrast with the background that the component is on"; a focus indicator on "the border of the component (inside the component and adjacent to the outside) ... need[s] to contrast with both adjacent colors"; "partly inside and partly outside, where either part of the focus indicator can contrast". CONFIRMED.
- Inactive: exempting "inactive controls ... was based on a number of considerations"; "a one-size-fits-all solution has been very difficult to establish. A method of varying the presentation of disabled controls, such as adding an icon for disabled controls, based on user preferences is anticipated". CONFIRMED.
- 2.4.13 Focus Appearance (AAA, new in 2.2): "an area of the focus indicator meets all the following: is at least as large as the area of a 2 CSS pixel thick perimeter of the unfocused component or sub-component, and has a contrast ratio of at least 3:1 between the same pixels in the focused and unfocused states." Exceptions: indicator "determined by the user agent", or indicator and its background "not modified by the author". Sufficient techniques include "C40: Creating a two-color focus indicator to ensure sufficient contrast with all components" and "C41: Creating a strong focus indicator within the component"; for image or gradient grounds, "a solid box with a border to guarantee there is sufficient contrast across variations of background images or background gradients." CONFIRMED.
- Reading: COL-2 (3:1 against every adjacent colour for a needed boundary, state mark or icon; a boundary is needed only when nothing else shows the control; hover need not contrast with rest). COL-86 (an alpha edge must hit 3:1 on each ground it lands on; thin lines render fainter). SUR-5 (a state change shown by colour alone between non-adjacent states is not held to 3:1, so width or another cue carries it). STA-3 and STA-7 (2 px perimeter, 3:1 change; a two-colour ring is a named technique). COL-65 (inactive exempt).

CONFIRMED.
- Accessibility concerns: "web authors should not be using the forced-colors media feature to create a separate design for users with this feature enabled. Instead, its intended usage is to make small tweaks". CONFIRMED.

- The transparent-outline pattern is not on either MDN page. It is in govuk-frontend source (R18) and implied by the rule above: an outline's colour is forced to a system colour, so a `transparent` outline becomes visible.
