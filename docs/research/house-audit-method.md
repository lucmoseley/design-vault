## Item 1. The dependency order: function, then role, then treatment, then value

**Value and place.** `docs/research/principle-interactions.md:45`-`:54` (§1 table); #154 body ("Precedence (#103 Q5 (A), #121)"); `plugin/skills/design-critique/references/house-principles.md:9`-`:24` (untracked draft, labelled "House synthesis ... confidence 0.7"). Two parts: (a) the four steps in this sequence; (b) "A later step never changes an earlier one. ... If a later step and an earlier step disagree, the earlier step wins."

**Ruling.** #103 Q5 (A), 14 Oct 2026, the agent's recommendation, accepted with the rest of the round ("The founder accepted every recommendation").

**What the sources say.**
- Garrett, "The Elements of User Experience" diagram, 30 March 2000 (http://www.jjg.net/elements/pdf/elements.pdf, read in full). It orders the elements from "Abstract" to "Concrete" on an axis marked "time ... Conception ... Completion": user needs and site objectives, then functional specifications and content requirements, then interaction design and information architecture, then interface, navigation and information design, then "Visual Design: graphic treatment of interface elements (the "look" in "look-and-feel")". That is function before treatment, the same direction as the house. The diagram also says: "this model does not describe a development process".
- Garrett on the dependency direction, in his own words (The WELL, Inkwell topic 186, post 26; https://people.well.com/conf/inkwell.vue/topics/186/Jesse-James-Garrett-The-Elements-page02.html, read through the fetch tool): "smart visual design solutions can only succeed when they're built on smart choices about the underlying planes", and "there's a ripple effect up and down the planes, as choices made in one area create inevitable consequences in others." He says the real process must "allow for those interdependencies, and build a degree of flexibility into the process."
- Material 3 tokens (material-components/material-web `docs/theming/README.md`, read through `gh api`): "Reference tokens hold concrete values, such as a hex color, pixel size, or font family name." "System tokens define decisions and roles". "Each component token maps to a system token, which has a concrete reference value." This supports the split of role (step 2) from value (step 4): a role is named once and a value fills it. The same three tiers are general practice (primitive or option, semantic or decision, component); I read this only in a secondary article (alwaystwisted.com, 23 Apr 2026). Nathan Curtis's own article returned HTTP 403.
- Norman: a signifier is "some sort of indicator, some signal in the physical or social world that can be interpreted meaningfully" (Interactions 15(6), Nov-Dec 2008; search summary only, the paper was not opened). This supports step 2 (a signifier carries a function). It gives no four-step order.
- Sullivan, "form ever follows function" (Lippincott's, March 1896): from knowledge, not re-read. It is a slogan, not an order of design steps.
- No source found that names the four steps function, role, treatment, value in this sequence. No source found that orders them in the opposite direction as a rule. A value-first practice exists (a brand palette, or M3's dynamic colour that makes roles from one seed colour), but in it the roles stay fixed and the values fill them, so it does not reverse the house order.

**Disposition.** SOURCED for part (a), the direction: Garrett 2000 (abstract to concrete, visual design last) and Material 3's role-over-value token tiers. The exact four steps are the house's synthesis of these. **Part (b) CONFLICTS in a small way with Garrett: he says consequences ripple "up and down the planes", so a concrete choice can force a rethink of an abstract one. The house says a later step "never" changes an earlier one.** The departure is small, because the house rule decides a clash between two rules at critique time, and Garrett speaks about the design process. What research supports: the earlier step wins a clash by default, and a later-step finding may send an earlier decision back for review (to the founder or the brief), not be silently dropped. The draft `house-principles.md:22` already does part of this: it reports a one-look-across-functions clash to the founder.

**Confidence.** 0.75 that the direction is sourced. 0.6 that "never" is a real departure and not only a wording difference. Would move it: the text of Garrett 2011 pp. 22-23 ("dependencies of, 22-23" in the book's index; the Pearson sample holds chapter 4 only) on whether an upper plane may force a change in a lower plane.

**Disposition.** SOURCED for level 1 at the top and for level 2 over levels 5-6. **CONFLICTS for level 2 over level 3 where the project rule sets behaviour, not look: research-led practice (GOV.UK) asks for user-research evidence before a local rule departs from an evidenced pattern; the house asks only that the rules file "record why".** What research supports: level 2 wins on the look (value systems, brand); on behaviour, a project rule beats level 3 only with evidence of its own, or else it goes to the founder as a named clash. The "WCAG 2.2" label: NOT A DESIGN CLAIM (a scope choice past the legal 2.1 floor); state it as such.

**Confidence.** 0.8 on the GOV.UK conflict (primary text read). 0.6 that it matters in practice: Manta's rules file mostly sets look. ## Item 5. The review date: "a year after writing, a guess"

**Value and place.** `plugin/skills/design-critique/references/surface-styling.md:5` and `text-presentation.md:5`: "A review date is a year after writing, a guess." **What the sources say.**

- Clinical guidelines, measured. Shekelle et al., JAMA 2001;286:1461-7 (PubMed abstract, read): "about half the guidelines were outdated in 5.8 years ...
