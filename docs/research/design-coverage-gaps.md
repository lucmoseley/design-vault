## W1. Accessibility standard today: WCAG 2.2, and contrast after WCAG 2

- **WCAG 2.2 became a W3C Recommendation on 5 October 2023.** It adds 9 criteria. Six apply at A or AA, which is the level that laws cite: 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum, AA); 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (AA); **2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum, AA): "at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels", or a listed exception such as spacing**; 3.2.6 Consistent Help (A); 3.3.7 Redundant Entry (A); 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum, AA). It removes 4.1.1 Parsing as obsolete. Source: W3C WAI, "What's New in WCAG 2.2", https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/ (read 13 Oct 2026). **Primary.**
- **WCAG 3 has no settled contrast method.** APCA left the WCAG 3 draft in July 2023 under the W3C rule that removes exploratory content after six months without working-group support. The editor's draft of 8 April 2026 says "The contrast algorithm used in WCAG 3 is yet to be determined." The author expects WCAG 3 about 2030 at the earliest. Advice: you can use APCA as a design aid, but pick colours that also pass WCAG 2, or document a failure. Source: Adrian Roselli, "WCAG3 Contrast as of April 2026", https://adrianroselli.com/2026/04/wcag3-contrast-as-of-april-2026.html, which cites the W3C editor's draft (https://w3c.github.io/wcag3/guidelines/#dfn-contrast-ratio-test) and the W3C maturity-labelling page. **Strong secondary** (recognised accessibility expert; the W3C pages it quotes are primary, but I did not open them).
- Era label: **era-bound, current.** WCAG 2.2 AA is the 2023-onward bar. A rule set written against WCAG 2.0/2.1 misses the six A/AA criteria above.

House match:
- impeccable names none of the WCAG 2.2 criteria by number. grep for "WCAG 2.2", "obscured", "Accessible Authentication", "Redundant Entry", "Consistent Help" returns 0 files. Its touch target is 44x44px (`audit.md`, `adapt.md`), which is stricter than 2.5.8 and so passes it. `harden.md` mentions dragging. **Partly covered.**
- Manta's brand sheet measures every pair against WCAG 2 ratios and cites WCAG 1.4.11. It does not cite 2.2. Its focus-ring rules (§10) do not cover 2.4.11 (a sticky bar or sheet that hides the focused control). Manta has a sticky compare bar and a sticky mobile handover bar (brand sheet §12a), so 2.4.11 applies directly. **Gap for Manta.**
- APCA: no house file uses or mentions it (grep: 0 in impeccable, 0 in Manta). Manta's "white on coral 3.0:1 is acceptable at 19px/700" (brand sheet §4) is correct under WCAG 2 (large text is 18.66px at 700). It is a choice that APCA would likely judge less kindly for a light-on-saturated-orange pair; this is my inference, not measured. **Not a gap to fill now** (WCAG 2 is the legal bar); an optional cross-check.

## W2. Performance: Core Web Vitals

- The three Core Web Vitals and their "good" limits: **LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1, at the 75th percentile of page loads, mobile and desktop apart.** INP became a stable Core Web Vital in 2024 and replaced FID. Source: web.dev, "Web Vitals", https://web.dev/articles/vitals (read 13 Oct 2026). **Primary.**
- Era label: **era-bound, current.** Any rule that says "FID < 100 ms" or "page load under 3 s" as the target is dated.

House match: impeccable `optimize.md` has the current three thresholds and notes the INP change. It runs only when `audit` scores performance below 3 of 4 (vault `references/impeccable.md`). Manta `image-to-video-hero.md` §B1 uses web.dev for LCP. The design-critique skill does not ask for a field or lab measurement; it relies on impeccable's code-reading audit. **Covered by impeccable; no measured gate in the house.**

## W3. The fold, hidden navigation, carousels and flat signifiers: what still holds

- **The fold.** NN/g eyetracking, 2018: about **57%** of page-viewing time was above the fold, 74% in the first two screenfuls; in 2010 it was 80% above the fold. Advice: put high-priority content and major CTAs at the top, avoid "false floors" (layouts that look finished), and give a cue that more content follows. Source: Fessenden, "Scrolling and Attention", NN/g, 15 Apr 2018, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/. **Primary** (first-party study). Note: the newest NN/g figure found is from 2018.
  - Era label: the fold as a hard line ("everything must fit above it") is **dated**. Priority at the top is **timeless**. The false-floor risk is **current**, and the full-screen hero makes it worse.
  - House match: design-critique §2 asks "above the fold, can a reader say what this is…". That is the 5-second test and agrees with the evidence. **No house rule names the false floor.** Partly covered.
- **Hidden navigation (hamburger).** NN/g, 2016, 179 participants: hidden navigation cut content discoverability by more than 20%; on desktop, 27% used a hidden menu against 48% for visible navigation, and tasks took at least 39% longer. Advice: on desktop, never hide the main navigation; on mobile, show links when there are four or fewer top-level items, and hide some only when there are more. Source: Pernice and Budiu, "Hamburger Menus and Hidden Navigation Hurt UX Metrics", NN/g, 26 Jun 2016, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/. **Primary**, but 10 years old; no newer NN/g finding was found.
  - Era label: "hamburger on desktop as a clean look" is a **dated 2014–2017 trend**. Visible or combo navigation is **current** advice.
  - House match: impeccable `adapt.md` says "Transform complex nav to hamburger/drawer on mobile" and "hamburger + drawer on mobile", with no item-count test. Manta's `DESIGN.md` collapses links behind a "Menu" button below 768px and keeps them inline on desktop, which agrees with NN/g. **Partly covered; impeccable's default is looser than the evidence.**
- **Carousels.** Baymard: "46% of all homepage carousels on ecommerce sites have UX performance issues". Advice: **no auto-rotation on mobile**; on desktop, rotate at 5–7 s (up to 10 s for text-heavy slides), pause on hover, and stop for good after a manual change; static content sections perform about as well and are simpler. Source: Baymard, "Homepage Carousels", first published 30 Apr 2019, updated 3 Apr 2025, https://baymard.com/blog/homepage-carousel. **Primary.**
  - Era label: the auto-rotating homepage hero slider is a **dated 2010s convention**. A user-driven image carousel inside a listing card (Manta `DESIGN.md` listing card) is a different pattern and is not the homepage slider.
  - House match: impeccable `adapt.md` mentions "swipe gestures where appropriate (lists, carousels)". WCAG 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide) covers auto-motion but no house file names it. **No house rule on hero carousels.** Gap (low frequency, because the house's taste avoids them).
- **Flat design and signifiers.** NN/g eyetracking, 71 users, 9 page pairs: pages with weak (flat) signifiers took about 22% longer and had about 25% more fixations. Flat design works when density is low, the layout is conventional, and targets have high contrast. Source: Moran, "Flat UI Elements Attract Less Attention and Cause Uncertainty", NN/g, 3 Sep 2017, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/flat-ui-less-attention-cause-uncertainty/. **Primary.**
  - Era label: **ultra-flat (2013–2016) is dated**; heavy skeuomorphism (to 2013) is dated. Current practice is "flat 2.0": flat surfaces, but clear signifiers on every control (underlined links, button shape, contrast).
  - House match: Manta is "Flat UI: borders first, shadow on hover" and keeps strong signifiers (coral CTA, outline buttons, underlined links on ink). impeccable craft-floor demands states and real controls. No house rule says "a link or button must look clickable at rest" in words. **Partly covered.**

## W4. Perception, Gestalt and the first impression (timeless)

- **The grouping principles are wider than six.** The standard review lists the classical principles (proximity, similarity, **common fate**, good continuation, closure, **symmetry**, **parallelism**) and newer ones (**synchrony**, common region, **element and uniform connectedness**), plus figure-ground organisation. Source: Wagemans et al., "A Century of Gestalt Psychology in Visual Perception: I. Perceptual Grouping and Figure-Ground Organization", *Psychological Bulletin* 138(6):1172–1217, 2012, abstract via PubMed 22845751 / PMC3482144. **Strong secondary (peer-reviewed review); abstract read, full text not read.**
  - Era label: **timeless.**
  - House match: design-critique §2 walks six (proximity, similarity, closure, continuity, common region, figure/ground). It omits **common fate and synchrony** (things that move together group together), which matter for any page with motion and for brand motion, and **uniform connectedness** (a line or shared fill that joins elements is the strongest grouping cue, stronger than proximity). impeccable has no "Gestalt" word (grep: 0 files); its layout command uses proximity and the squint test. **Partly covered; common fate is the gap that matters most, because the founder makes motion.**
- **First impressions form in 17–50 ms, and simple + familiar wins.** In two studies (119 real screenshots), visual complexity and prototypicality (how much a site looks like its category) changed aesthetic ratings within 50 ms, and within 17 ms for complexity. **Sites with low visual complexity and high prototypicality were rated most appealing.** Source: Tuch, Presslaber, Stöcklin, Opwis and Bargas-Avila, "The role of visual complexity and prototypicality regarding first impression of websites", *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies* 70(11):794–811, 2012; Google Research page https://research.google/pubs/pub38315/. **Strong secondary (peer-reviewed); abstract read.**
  - Era label: **timeless** as a mechanism; what counts as "prototypical" for a category is **era-bound** and moves with the market.
  - House match: **gap.** No house file names visual complexity or prototypicality. This finding is in tension with the house's anti-sameness rules (House DNA `never`, impeccable `concept-seed`, the "too generic" repair): it says that a page that breaks its category's layout pays a first-impression cost. The way through, from the same evidence: be distinctive in brand assets (colour, type, illustration, voice) and keep the page's structure prototypical for its category. impeccable's Operate and Read modes already say this ("The world lends four things and only these: type, palette, density, and one signature move", `mode-operate.md`). Its Persuade mode pushes the other way ("a composition the category would not attempt", `mode-persuade.md`). **No house rule states the trade-off for Persuade pages. A small-business site (Persuade mode) is where it matters.**

## W5. Body type size and dark mode

- **Body size.** Butterick: "For websites, I recommend body text of 15–25 pixels." He calls the old small web sizes a relic of 1990s screens: "It's time to let it go." Source: Butterick, *Practical Typography*, "Point size", https://practicaltypography.com/point-size.html (read 13 Oct 2026). **Strong secondary.**
  - Era label: **era-bound.** 12–14px body text is a **dated 1990s–2000s convention**. Current practice is 16px or more for running text, and up to about 18–20px for reading pages.
  - House match: impeccable `typeset.md` sets "1rem / 16px as the ordinary web body floor". **Manta's brand sheet §4 and `DESIGN.md` set body at 14–15.5px (token 15px).** That is at or below the bottom of Butterick's range, and below impeccable's floor; the brand sheet gives no source or reason for it. For a scan-heavy results list a 14–15px secondary line is defensible; for guide pages (Read mode) it is a dated default. **Flag: a Manta rule on a dated convention.** **Partly covered (by impeccable).**
- **Dark mode.** Apple HIG: people "generally expect all apps and games to respect their preference"; "Avoid offering an app-specific appearance setting"; contrast "no lower than 4.5:1… strive for… 7:1, especially in small text"; dark-mode colours "aren't necessarily inversions"; base and elevated background sets show depth. Source: Apple Human Interface Guidelines, "Dark Mode", read via https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode.json (13 Oct 2026). **Primary.** NN/g, 2 Feb 2020: "in users with normal vision, light mode leads to better performance most of the time"; dark mode can help some low-vision users (cloudy ocular media); do not make dark the default for a general audience, but let users switch, and follow the OS setting. Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/. **Primary (literature review by NN/g).**
  - Era label: **current (2019 onward).** `prefers-color-scheme` support is now expected. "Dark by default because it looks premium" is a **trend, not evidence**.
  - House match: impeccable `colorize.md` ("Light and dark themes are each composed, not mechanically inverted") and craft-floor ("Light or dark picked by category… Pick it from the use scene"). Manta: day by default, night ground only in named contexts; **no rule on whether Manta answers `prefers-color-scheme`** (grep "dark mode" in Manta: only `motion-reference-capture.md`). **Partly covered; a decision gap for Manta, not a craft gap.**

## W6. Imagery and art direction

- **Users look at information-carrying images and ignore decorative ones.** Nielsen's eyetracking: real staff portraits got about 10% more viewing time than bios that took 316% more space; a decorative photo on an application page was "pure filler"; "jazzed-up = ignored". Source: Nielsen, "Photos as Web Content", NN/g, 31 Oct 2010, reviewed 13 Aug 2026, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/. **Primary; old study, re-reviewed by NN/g in 2026.**
  - Era label: **timeless** (task-relevance). The generic stock photo of smiling people is a **dated 2000s–2010s convention**. The current equivalent is the generic AI-generated hero image, with the same "filler" problem.
  - House match: House DNA bans "a stock photo of a smiling group" and "a stock photo used as found". design-round says "Label artwork where a viewer could mistake it for product evidence". Manta: photography on listings (task-relevant), illustration for brand surfaces, and a logged hypothesis that illustration heroes on SEO pages may underperform photos (brand sheet §9, §14 item 6). **Covered in taste; the evidence rule ("an image earns its place by carrying information or a deliberate feeling, else it is filler") is not written down.** For a solo advisor's site, the real portrait of the person is the most-viewed image on the page (Nielsen above)

## W7. Search, filters and maps on an accommodation site (Manta's working area)

- Baymard's accommodation study (published 1 Feb 2022): 992 hours of testing, 317+ moderated think-aloud sessions, 1,000+ medium-to-severe issues, 364 guidelines, across OTAs, hotel chains, boutique hotels and rental sites. Three takeaways: (1) the date-and-guest "booking" search is the critical feature and some sites give it too little prominence; (2) pricing is complex, and users drop properties whose final cost "suddenly contains new or unexplained fees"; (3) users want to make special requests or contact the host. Users want relevant, scannable filters, and often room or bed-type filters, not only price and rating. Source: Scott, Baymard, https://baymard.com/blog/new-research-travel-accommodations. **Primary.**
- Baymard, "The Optimal Layout for Hotel & Property Rental Search Results" (8 Feb 2022): always show results in a **split view, list beside an interactive map**; 70% of OTA and hotel-chain sites defaulted to a list with no map, and 65% of users on those sites did not use a map; on Hilton's split view 95% of users used the map. Pitfalls: a split view put in an overlay; **vertical filter sidebars that crowd the list (horizontal filter and sort bars performed well)**; vertical ads. The article does not cover mobile. Source: Collins, Baymard, https://baymard.com/blog/accommodations-split-view. **Primary.** Baymard's audit pages also say 40% of sites lack popular industry-specific filters and 61% do not promote important filters (from search snippets of https://baymard.com/audits/travel-accommodations and https://baymard.com/blog/promoting-product-filters; **not opened, treat as secondary**).
- Era label: **current** (2022 study, still Baymard's live guidance). Timeless part: location is a primary comparison attribute for lodging.

House match:
- Manta's `DESIGN.md` and brand sheet specify the listing card in detail (photo-led, score before price, facts line, one action zone) and tax-inclusive prices. They carry **no rule for the results page as a whole**: no map/list layout, no filter-bar placement, no sort control, no empty-results state for filters, no mobile results pattern. A `map-view` card exists in the Manta tracker (issue #2988 names it and its bottom sheet), so the map is planned, but no design research backs its layout. Manta's competitor teardown describes Hostelworld's filters but does not judge them against usability evidence.
- The vault and impeccable have no accommodation or search-results guidance. impeccable's Persuade mode says the opening must expose the category's real action, "an airline's route-and-dates search" (`mode-persuade.md`), which agrees with Baymard's takeaway 1. **Gap — highest-frequency gap for the hostel site.**

## W8. Conversion, trust and honest pricing

- NN/g's four trust factors (8 May 2016): design quality; **upfront disclosure** (contact, costs, fees, policies); comprehensive, correct and current content; connection to the rest of the web (reviews, social, news). Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/. **Primary**; 10 years old, but the factors are **timeless**.
- **US FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees (16 CFR Part 464), in effect from 12 May 2025**, covers **short-term lodging** and live-event tickets. A business that shows a price must show the total price with all mandatory fees; government taxes may be disclosed later but before payment; the rule applies to "any… entity or individual" that offers, displays or advertises short-term lodging. Sources: FTC press release https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/05/ftc-rule-unfair-or-deceptive-fees-take-effect-may-12-2025 (search snippet) and FTC business blog, 2024-12, https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2024/12/getting-bottom-line-ftcs-bipartisan-junk-fees-rule-your-business (read). **Primary.** Whether the rule binds a non-US hostel-discovery site that shows a "from" price to US visitors was not researched.
- FTC staff report "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" (Sept 2022) names fake countdown timers and false scarcity or demand claims as dark patterns. Source: law-firm summaries found by search (Arnold & Porter, Venable); the report itself not opened. **Secondary.**
- Era label: **current.** Countdown timers, "only 2 left" and drip pricing were common 2010s conversion tactics; they are now regulated deceptive patterns in the US (and under EU consumer law, not researched here).

House match:
- Manta is **ahead of the evidence**: urgency theatre banned, tax-inclusive prices declared once, no fabricated proof, source lines on facts, an honest score scale, and testimonials never dressed as reviews (brand sheet §3, §7, §11, §12a; `DESIGN.md`). design-critique §2 asks for risk-reversal, objections, credibility, and refuses invented proof. **Covered for Manta.**
- For other projects the vault has only design-critique §2 and House DNA's "fabricated proof" ban. **"Upfront disclosure" (contact details, prices, policies, who is behind the business) is named in design-critique as "Credibility, and who is behind the product"; partly covered.** For a solo advisor's site, this is the main trust lever, and no house file says what it must show (a real name and face, credentials, location, contact route, fees or "how I am paid").

## W9. Motion standards

- **WCAG 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions (AAA):** "Motion animation triggered by interaction can be disabled", unless essential. The Understanding page names parallax scrolling as a common non-essential animation and says motion can trigger vestibular reactions (dizziness, nausea, headaches). `prefers-reduced-motion` (technique C39) is a sufficient technique. Source: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html. **Primary.** WCAG 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide (A) covers motion that starts by itself (from the WCAG 2.2 text; the 5-second detail not re-read here).
- **Material 3 moved its motion system to springs in 2025 ("M3 Expressive").** The Compose API reference says: "The expressive scheme is Material's recommended motion scheme for prominent UI elements and hero interactions… The standard scheme is Material's basic motion scheme for utilitarian UI elements and recurring interactions"; spatial specs (position, size) are separate from effects specs (colour, opacity). Source: search snippets of https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/compose/material3/MotionScheme and the M3 blog https://m3.material.io/blog/m3-expressive-motion-theming (both pages render by script; their text was not read in full). **Primary, read by snippet only.** M3's older easing-and-duration tokens page (https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/easing-and-duration) is still published.
- Era label: purpose-led motion, interruption, reduced-motion alternatives are **current**. Scroll-jacking and heavy parallax are **dated 2012–2016 trends** with an accessibility cost. "Never spring" is **no longer the platform norm**: the two big systems (Apple's SwiftUI springs; M3 Expressive) now use springs for spatial motion, with low or no bounce for utilitarian UI.

House match:
- impeccable `animate.md` is current: motion has a job, duration table, exits faster, View Transitions, scroll-driven motion only when meaningful, reduced motion as an alternative, not a kill switch. design-round's motion brief requires "the reduced-motion version" and "how to skip it". The craft gate's rule 3 and brand-motion easing use damped springs for brand motion. **Covered.**
- Manta: "no bounce or spring theatre", 120/180 ms, one easing curve. This is a brand choice and is consistent with M3's "standard" (low-bounce) scheme for utilitarian UI. It is not dated. Note: brand sheet §10 has no clause for a drag that settles (Manta issue #2988 says so), which is where a spring is the platform norm. **Known open item, owned by Manta #2988.**
- **Gap:** no house file names parallax or scroll-jacking as a vestibular risk, or WCAG 2.2.2 for auto-playing motion (a looping hero video, a marquee, an auto-rotating carousel). Manta's video plan (design-process.md) gates on `prefers-reduced-motion`, but a visible pause control for a loop over 5 s is not stated.

## W10. Responsive and device design, and the current CSS platform

- **Baseline** (web.dev): "newly available" means supported in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari; "widely available" means 30 months after that. Source: https://web.dev/baseline. **Primary.**
- Size **container queries: Baseline newly available February 2023**; **`:has()`: December 2023**; **same-document View Transitions: 14 October 2025** (Firefox 144); **cross-document View Transitions: not yet Baseline** (an Interop 2026 focus area). Sources: web.dev "Baseline 2023" https://web.dev/blog/baseline2023 and web.dev blog on view transitions https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available, read through search snippets. **Primary, snippet level.**
- Touch targets: WCAG 2.5.8 AA asks 24×24 CSS px; Apple asks 44×44 pt; Material asks 48×48 dp (Apple and Material figures from memory, **not re-read in this session**).
- **Google completed mobile-first indexing in October 2023**: Google crawls and indexes the mobile version. Source: Google Search Central blog, "Mobile-first indexing has landed", https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/10/mobile-first-is-here (search snippet). **Primary.**
- Era label: device-width breakpoints ("iPhone, iPad, desktop") and a separate m-dot site are **dated 2010–2015 conventions**. Content-driven breakpoints, fluid type with `clamp()`, and container queries are **current**. Hover-only affordances are **dated** (touch laptops). "Mobile-first" is **current** and now also an indexing fact.

House match:
- impeccable `adapt.md` and `layout.md`: content-driven breakpoints, container queries, pointer and hover media queries, 44px targets, no hover-only functions, `<picture>` art direction, evidence of what device was tested. design-round renders desktop, an intermediate width and mobile. **Covered.**

## W11. Content, microcopy, readability and forms

- **Plain language.** GOV.UK content design aims at a reading age of 9 and says plain English also applies to specialist readers (from secondary pages that quote it: Somerset County Council playbook and others found by search; the GOV.UK page itself redirected and its text was not read). **Secondary.** Era label: **timeless.**
- **Forms.** Baymard (26 Jun 2024): the average checkout has 11.3 form fields; "most sites need only 8"; 17% of users have abandoned an order because of checkout complexity. Source: https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-flow-average-form-fields. **Primary.** Baymard's inline-validation research: validate when the user leaves a field, not while typing; clear the error once fixed; 32% of sites validate nothing (search snippet of https://baymard.com/research-articles/inline-form-validation). **Primary, snippet level.**
- Era label: placeholder-as-label and "validate on submit only" are **dated 2010s conventions**; persistent labels, inline validation on blur, and fewer fields are **current**.

House match:

## W13. Brand expression, distinctiveness and the "AI look"

- **Distinctive brand assets are measured, not judged by eye.** Romaniuk's framework scores each asset (colour, mark, character, type, sound) on **fame** (share of category buyers who name the brand from the asset alone) and **uniqueness** (whether other brands use it too); the Ehrenberg-Bass grid sorts assets into use-or-lose, invest, and avoid. A 2023 *Journal of Brand Management* study (Brus, Hartnett, Faulkner, Driesener) found marketers overestimate fame and underestimate sharing. Sources: Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* (OUP, 2018); Marketing Week report of the 2023 study; both found by search, not read. **Strong secondary (book and peer-reviewed paper), read at summary level only.** Era label: **timeless** (marketing science).
- **Evidence on AI-generated design sameness is thin.** The one academic paper found is conceptual: Shin, Gao, Pang, Lee, Reinecke and Tseng, "Interrogating Design Homogenization in Web Vibe Coding", arXiv:2603.13036, 13 Mar 2026: "the push for frictionless generation can exacerbate homogenization and its harms"; it proposes "productive friction" and reports no measurement. **Weak-to-moderate (preprint, no data).** A 2025 Stanford / Imperial / Internet Archive study, as reported by Decrypt, measured higher **semantic** similarity in AI-written pages but did **not** confirm a stylistic monoculture. **Secondary (press report), not read at source.** Journalism lists the look (glow effects, purple-blue palettes, italic serif headings, monospace numerals). **Weak.**
  - Era label: **era-bound, 2023 onward.** The "AI look" is the current generic default, as the Bootstrap look (about 2012–2016) and the flat "corporate Memphis" illustration style (about 2017–2021) were before it.
- House match:
  - House DNA `never` (12 lines), Manta's "AI-slop markers" in the Guardrails prefill, impeccable craft-floor's refuse list and `concept-seed`'s random direction, and design-round's "too generic" repair. **Covered as taste, well beyond the published evidence.** The house's ban list is a set of observed tells, not a measured list; it will date as models change, so it needs a review date.
  - Manta's brand sheet §12 (differentiation from Hostelworld, verified Jul 2026) works in the Romaniuk way: it names the competitor's strongest cue (orange field) and keeps it out. **No house file uses fame/uniqueness as a test for a new client's brand assets.** For a small-business site, where the founder sets the brand from nothing (mmm-infra), this is the method gap behind "distinctive": the critique cannot say whether a mark or colour will be remembered as this brand.

## W14. Accessibility law (context for client sites)

- **European Accessibility Act** (Directive (EU) 2019/882) applies from **28 June 2025** to listed services, which include consumer e-commerce. **Microenterprises that provide services (fewer than 10 staff and turnover or balance sheet ≤ €2 million) are exempt**; national laws such as Germany's BFSG point to EN 301 549 (WCAG 2.1 AA). Sources: consultancy and law-firm summaries found by search (Reed Smith, e-include.eu, others); EUR-Lex returned no text to the fetch tool. **Secondary; check the directive before relying on it.**
- Era label: **current.**
- House match: **no house file mentions accessibility law.** For the founder's projects as described (an Indonesia-based hostel site, a solo advisor), the EAA's microenterprise exemption probably applies, so the practical bar stays WCAG 2.2 AA as good practice. Low frequency as a legal driver; not researched for the US (ADA) or Indonesia.
