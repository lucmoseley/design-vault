# Research: the founder's design rejections, rule breach or taste (design-vault #102, map #96)

Question: In the design rounds run so far, when the founder rejected or changed a candidate, was it a breach of a written rule that a critic could have caught, or taste that no rule held at that time?

Corpus: (1) Manta design rounds in lucmoseley/mantahostels issues and `~/mantahostels/docs/`. (2) mmm-infra rounds, from the research notes in `docs/research/mmm-infra-*.md` of this repo, and mmm-infra issues for exact words.

Status: complete (13 Oct 2026).

## Findings

### Method and unit of count

- **Unit.** One unit is one fault or one change that the founder named against a candidate (or against all candidates of a round). When he names the same fault on many drafts, it counts one time per round. A draft that he did not choose, and did not comment on, is not a unit.
- **Rules at the time.** For each unit, I quote the rule that was in force on the day of the round. I read the rule files at the commit before the round: design-vault `0202f18` (1 Oct, in force for the #9 animation rounds on 4-5 Oct) and `5d1f926` (5 Oct 15:31 +0800, in force for the #8 site-look round one, which started 13 minutes later, per `docs/research/mmm-infra-site-look-round.md` V0). mmm-infra `docs/brand/brand-sheet.md` at `44acf0f` (4 Oct). House DNA: `dna` in `data/gallery.json`, 12 `never` lines and 0 `constants`, the same in every commit since 20 Jul.
- **Critic status.** "Saw" means a critic report or round-record strip names the fault. "Filed low" means it names the fault but calls it not a defect, or does not fail the candidate. "Missed" means no critic text that I read names it. "Unknown" means I did not read the critic report for that draft.
- **Classes** (from the dispatch): (1) rule breach missed; (2) rule breach filed low; (3) direction or taste with no rule; (4) factual or build error; (5) other. In class 5 I name the reason. Two reasons occur often enough to name them: **5a, the founder rejected the written rule itself**, and **5b, process or tooling fault, not a judgement of the candidate**.

**Sample-size caveat.** The sample is small and not independent. 23 of the 28 main units come from one client project (mmm-infra) and four rounds in two days (4-6 Oct). One long founder reply (mmm-infra#9 issuecomment-5988390174) gives 7 units. Manta gives no round at all. The unit (one named fault per round) is my choice; a per-draft count would raise class 4 and class 3 together and change the shares by a few points, not their order. All units come from one founder, and the rules in force changed in the middle of the sample (`5d1f926`, 5 Oct). So the shares describe these rounds; they do not predict the next ones with any precision.

**What the counts say.**
1. **A rule-conformance check would have caught very little.** Classes 1 and 2 are 3 of 28 (11%, 4-27%). Of these, B2 and B7 were already seen by the critics and kept alive by a gate that did not test client fit, or by the session ("belongs to the layout round"). The fix that those two need is narrow: make the client's written facts (audience, "the individual is the brand") a gate that can fail a tile. The site-look note S2 proposes this. No unit shows a critic that read a rule and failed to apply it to a candidate.
2. **Most rejections are taste that no rule held then.** Class 3 is half the main sample (14 of 28). It splits two ways. Motion and geometry craft (A6 flow, with the class 4 faults A2, A3, A11) were new standards that the founder stated once and wanted "permanent enforced rules for any project". Look and register (B1 childish, B2/B8 busy and techy, B5/B6 nothing right or boring, B9 frame) are direct taste verdicts on one tile.
3. **Build errors are the second-largest class** (6 of 28, 21%). ## Answer

For the rounds run so far, the founder's rejections were mostly **taste that no rule held then** (14 of 28, 50%, interval 33-67%) and **build errors that a measured check can catch** (6 of 28, 21%, 10-40%). Written-rule breaches were rare (3 of 28, 11%, 4-27%). In 2 of those 3 a critic saw the breach and the round kept the tile alive, so the cause was a missing client-fit gate, not a missing rule. In 3 more units (11%) the founder rejected the written rule itself, which a stricter rule-by-rule check would have enforced against him.

So the evidence points to **unwritten taste** as the larger problem: a corrections log, and calibration of the critic against his verdicts. Two narrow, rule-side items also have support: a client-fit gate that can fail a tile on the client's written facts (B2, B7), and the measured craft checks that already exist for build errors.  Manta has no round yet; its #3059 round one will be the first test outside mmm-infra.
