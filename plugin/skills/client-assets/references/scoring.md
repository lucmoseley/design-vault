# Scoring client assets

These rules bind every scoring sheet `client-assets` builds: a written rubric that scores client assets for their uses. Whoever applies it (a critic, blind scorers or the session) is a scorer below. The project's own values (its uses, their bars, its rules and its ruled examples) live in its repo, never here.

## Building the sheet

1. **Vetoes per use, then rank.** Each use (a homepage hero, a team-page headshot, a printed flyer) states its own bar: the named faults that rule an asset out of that use. Apply the vetoes first, then rank the survivors within each use. A vetoed asset stays out of that use however strong its other scores for it: the founder judges by veto, then overall impression (design-vault#90 P11).
2. **Sources from the same field and use.** A rule's source judges the same kind of asset for the same kind of use. A rule borrowed from another field (stock-licence model releases, a bank's terms for its own logo) is a note on the sheet, never a veto. Every veto carries one sentence: "A viewer would reject this asset for this slot because …". A veto that cannot complete it becomes a note.
3. **Two-sided examples he ruled.** Every rule keeps at least one must-pass and one must-fail example, each ruled by the founder (or someone who knows the subject), never a model's note. When an example is dropped, mark the rule untested until a ruled one replaces it. Keep some of his rulings out of the rule-writer's sight (its brief, its files, the commit subjects it can read) as a held-out check (design-vault#90 P10).
4. **Audit every new rule** before it joins the sheet, as lucmoseley/mmm-infra#30 did: does the source say it; would it fault what a sensible viewer accepts, tested on named false positives; is it judged in the context of use. Then keep it, rewrite it or drop it.

## Scoring

5. **Saw the image.** A scorer states, per item, that it saw the image, and stops at the first one missing. A row written without its image is discarded (design-vault#90 P13).
6. **Read before the anchor.** A scorer records its own reading of an item before it sees any founder example, so its brief carries none. The example then checks the reading and never replaces it; shown first, it pulls the reading toward itself (design-vault#90 P12).
7. **Record what was judged.** Every score records the exact crop and display size it judged, and keeps "the file we hold" apart from "if the original arrives", both labelled.

## The founder's time

8. **Close calls only.** Ask the founder only about items whose answer could change a list: scorers split on whether a veto applies, or an item close to its use's cut-off (the last place on the list).
9. **Stop after two misses.** When a scorer misses one of his rulings twice on the same rule, stop rewriting the rule. Set that item to his ruling by hand, and spend his time on new items close to a cut-off.
