# Writing to <client> — the founder's messages to the client

Any message the founder sends <client> about this project is drafted by these rules: an email, a chat message, a reply to a question about their site. Site copy in <client>'s own voice is the rules file's §5 Voice, not this file.

**When the founder corrects a word or the tone in a draft to <client>, write the correction into this file, as a rule or into the client's words below, in the same turn.** (design-vault#89 S6)

Grades: **Strong**: two or more independent sources, one peer-reviewed, read from the primary text. **Moderate**: one good source. **Weak**: inference or practitioner view. **Ruling**: the founder's call.

## Rules

### 1. Answer only what they asked — Ruling

Give no fix they did not ask for, in words or by implication. Ask no question that serves our records (a caption fact, an original file, an event name); those go in a message of their own, when the work that needs them asks.

### 2. Every judgement carries its reason; a fault also says why it matters to them — Moderate

"A good choice - it's dynamic and you look engaged", not "is good". Praise or fault an item by what it does for them, not by craft terms alone. Never cut a reason to save length; cut a point instead. (Shute 2008, "Focus on formative feedback".)

### 3. Say "I think" on an opinion — Moderate

"I think" or "in my opinion" on a judgement (Oba, Berger & Boghrati 2026). A fact checked on the page goes unhedged; a fact they know better than us takes "that I can tell" (Ruling).

### 4. Name each item by where it sits on the page — Ruling

"The photo at the top of the homepage", "top row, middle". Never our ids, file names, dates or events: they will not know an item by those.

### 5. Their words, not the rubric's — Ruling

Use the words in <client>'s words for design faults above. Where the founder has already put a verdict in his own words, use those. Never a slot, a criterion, a score or a design term they would not use ("soft", "copy").

### 6. Length fits the question, not their message — Weak

An open question takes as many points as are worth making, each with its reason. Matching their length was rejected: the study behind it measured no outcome (Kooti et al. 2015).

### 7. A string check does not show a draft sounds like the founder — Moderate

The check is the voice sample above, then the founder's read (Jakesch, Hancock & Naaman 2023). No lint for cold business email applies here: its subtract-only rules cut the reasons rule 2 keeps (design-vault#89 S9).

### 8. Fault the picture, never the person — Ruling

"I don't think the one in the top left is a great picture of you", not "you've got an odd expression in it".

## Before showing a draft

Show the message alone, then below it every claim in it that was not checked on the live page or in a record, each marked "unverified".
